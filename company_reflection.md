# Company Reflection

## Why do I think Focus Bear was created?

After exploring the Focus Bear website, I understand the main idea as creating a tool for people who struggle with focus, distractions, routines, and getting started with everyday tasks.

What stood out to me is that Focus Bear was not created simply as another productivity app. It was built from lived experience, including the founder's experience with ADHD and autism. The idea of making routines easier to follow and reducing distractions feels more practical than simply telling people to "be more disciplined."

I think Focus Bear was created because traditional productivity systems do not work equally well for everyone. A person can know exactly what they need to do and still struggle to start, stay focused, or move from one task to another.

## What problem is Focus Bear solving?

The main problem I see is the gap between **knowing what you should do and actually being able to do it consistently**.

Distractions, difficult routines, procrastination, hyperfocus, and constantly switching between tasks can make studying or working much harder. Focus Bear tries to solve this by changing the environment around the user instead of relying only on willpower.

Features such as website and app blocking, guided routines, focus sessions, breaks, and step-by-step prompts are designed to make the desired behaviour easier.

## Why do I think this mission is important?

I think this mission is important because productivity is often treated as if everyone has the same brain and the same ability to concentrate.

Someone who struggles with focus can easily feel lazy or unmotivated, even when they are actually trying very hard. Having a tool that reduces distractions and breaks large routines into smaller steps can make the situation feel more manageable.

I also like the fact that the product seems to focus on helping rather than judging the user. The idea of a tool that "forgives" you when you slip is something I find more realistic than systems that expect perfect consistency.

## How does Focus Bear's work align with my personal values or interests?

As a student and someone interested in technology and software testing, I find Focus Bear interesting because it combines technology with a very human problem.

I am especially interested in products that are not only technically functional but also consider how real people interact with them. From a QA perspective, this is important because a feature can technically work correctly and still be difficult or frustrating for a particular user.

Focus Bear also made me think more about accessibility and inclusive design. Different users can experience the same product very differently, so testing should consider more than just whether a button works.

## Do I personally relate to any of the challenges Focus Bear aims to solve?

I can relate to some of these challenges even without assuming that everyone experiencing them has ADHD or autism.

As a university student, there are times when I know that I need to study or complete a task, but starting it can still be difficult. It is also easy to lose time by switching between different things, especially when studying involves a computer and the internet.

I think this is one reason the idea of reducing distractions interests me. Sometimes the problem is not a lack of knowledge about what I should be doing. The problem is having too many things competing for my attention at the same time.

I can also imagine how useful Focus Bear could be for someone who has more significant difficulties with executive functioning. Instead of giving that person another large list of things to remember, guiding them through one step at a time could make a routine feel much less overwhelming.

## How might Focus Bear help someone I know or someone without a diagnosis?

I think this is important because difficulties with focus and productivity are not limited to people with a formal diagnosis.

For example, a university student might struggle with starting assignments, remembering routines, staying away from social media, or maintaining concentration during long study sessions. They might not have ADHD or autism, but they could still benefit from having fewer distractions and a clearer structure.

For someone with ADHD or autism, these difficulties may be more significant and affect everyday life more consistently. Features such as distraction blocking, routines, breaks, and step-by-step guidance could therefore provide useful external structure.

What I find interesting is that the same feature can help different people for different reasons. Someone might use Focus Bear because they have ADHD, while another person might simply want fewer distractions while studying.

## My overall reflection

After looking at Focus Bear's website, I see it less as a simple productivity timer and more as a tool designed around how people actually behave.

The part that stood out to me most is the focus on reducing the amount of willpower and decision-making required from the user. I think that is a useful approach because struggling with productivity does not always mean that someone is lazy or does not care.

As someone interested in QA, this also reminds me that testing a product means thinking about the people who will actually use it. A feature should not only work technically; it should also make sense and be usable for the people it was designed to help.

**My main takeaway:** Technology is most useful when it adapts to real people's needs instead of expecting every person to work in exactly the same way.
