# Occupational Health & Safety (OHS) for Desk-Based Work: Laptop Ergonomics

## 1. Research & Learning

### Risks of Laptop-Only Work
Working directly on a laptop without external peripherals poses several musculoskeletal and physiological risks:
* **Neck and Cervical Spine Strain:** Looking down at a low screen creates "forward head posture," drastically increasing the effective weight of the head on the neck.
* **Shoulder and Upper Back Tension:** Hunched shoulders lead to muscle fatigue and chronic tension in the upper back.
* **Wrist Discomfort & RSI:** Typing directly on a raised, small laptop keyboard puts wrists at unnatural angles, increasing the risk of Repetitive Strain Injuries (RSI) like Carpal Tunnel Syndrome.
* **Eye Fatigue:** Small, improperly angled screens lead to squinting, dryness, and digital eye strain.

### Ergonomic Equipment
To improve posture and overall comfort during long desk sessions, the following equipment is essential:
* **Laptop Stand / Riser:** Elevates the screen to eye level.
* **External Keyboard & Mouse:** Allows hands and wrists to rest flat in a neutral position.
* **Ergonomic Chair:** Provides proper lumbar support for the lower back.
* **External Monitor:** Expands screen space and reduces neck rotation.

### Workspace Adjustments
* **Monitor Height:** Top third of the screen should be at eye level so eyes look slightly downward without tilting the head.
* **Chair Position:** Adjusted so feet are flat on the floor, hips slightly above knees, and lower back supported.
* **Desk Setup:** Elbows kept at a 90-to-100-degree angle, with wrists straight while typing.

### Daily Habits to Reduce Strain
* **20-20-20 Rule:** Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds to reduce eye fatigue.
* **Movement Breaks:** Stand up and stretch every 30 to 60 minutes.
* **Posture Audits:** Periodically check if shoulders are relaxed and back is straight.

## 3. Personal Reflection & Action Items

### Equipment & Behavioral Changes
* **Equipment Improvement:** I implemented a laptop stand along with an external keyboard and wireless mouse. (Future addition: an ergonomic chair with adjustable lumbar support).
* **Workspace Adjustment Made:** Raised the display height by 15 cm using the stand so that my neck remains neutral while reading.
* **Habit Adjustment:** Stopped keeping my legs crossed and started keeping both feet flat on the floor to align my spine properly.

### Focus Bear Experience
I used Focus Bear for posture and movement reminders throughout a full workday. 

* **What Changed:** Focus Bear enforced periodic break screens that forced me to step away, stretch my shoulders, and do quick eye-rest exercises.
* **Noticeable Improvements:** By the end of the day, I noticed a significant reduction in late-afternoon neck stiffness and lower back fatigue. The micro-breaks also helped refresh my mental focus, preventing the usual end-of-day burnout.
