# Professionalism Reflection

## 1. What Does Professionalism Mean?

Professionalism does not necessarily mean being formal or serious all the time. In a casual and remote environment, professionalism means being respectful, responsible, reliable, and considerate while still being able to have fun and be yourself.

### Professional Behaviour

Examples of professional behaviour include:

* Communicating clearly and respectfully.
* Being reliable and completing assigned tasks.
* Joining meetings on time and being prepared.
* Listening when others are speaking.
* Asking questions when something is unclear.
* Taking responsibility for mistakes.
* Giving constructive feedback.
* Respecting different opinions and working styles.
* Following through on commitments.
* Keeping teammates informed about progress or blockers.

### Unprofessional Behaviour

Examples of unprofessional behaviour include:

* Being disrespectful or dismissive toward teammates.
* Ignoring messages or important requests without explanation.
* Repeatedly missing meetings or deadlines without communicating.
* Interrupting or talking over other people.
* Blaming others instead of taking responsibility.
* Using insulting, discriminatory, or offensive language.
* Reacting aggressively to constructive feedback.
* Sharing confidential information inappropriately.
* Creating unnecessary conflict within the team.

A relaxed workplace can still have clear professional standards.

---

# 2. Respectful Communication in Remote Teams

Remote communication requires extra attention because written messages do not always communicate tone clearly.

I can make my communication more respectful by:

* Keeping messages clear and polite.
* Avoiding unnecessary assumptions about someone's intentions.
* Asking for clarification when something is unclear.
* Using a neutral and constructive tone during disagreements.
* Responding to important messages within a reasonable timeframe.
* Giving teammates enough context when asking for help.
* Informing others when I am blocked or unable to complete something on time.

For example, instead of:

> "This doesn't make sense."

I could say:

> "I'm not sure I understand this part. Could you clarify what the expected behaviour is?"

This keeps the communication focused on solving the problem rather than creating unnecessary conflict.

# 3. Giving and Receiving Feedback

## Giving Feedback

When giving feedback, I should focus on the work, behaviour, or outcome rather than the person.

A useful approach is:

1. Explain what I observed.
2. Explain why it matters.
3. Suggest an improvement when appropriate.
4. Give the other person an opportunity to respond.

For example:

> "I noticed that this test case does not include an invalid input scenario. Adding one could improve the coverage."

This is more constructive than simply saying:

> "Your test case is incomplete."

## Receiving Feedback

When receiving feedback, I should:

* Listen before responding.
* Avoid immediately becoming defensive.
* Ask questions if I do not understand the feedback.
* Consider whether the feedback identifies a genuine improvement.
* Apply useful feedback to future work.
* Thank the person for pointing out something I can improve.

Mistakes are a normal part of learning, especially during an internship.

# 4. Reflection on Teamwork

One example of effective teamwork is when team members communicate openly, share information, and help each other solve problems without blaming one another.

The most effective teamwork usually includes:

* Clear responsibilities.
* Good communication.
* Mutual respect.
* Trust.
* Constructive feedback.
* Willingness to ask for help.
* Accountability.

I believe a team can be both professional and fun. People should be able to joke, have conversations, and be themselves while still respecting boundaries and taking their responsibilities seriously.

---

# 5. How I Will Keep My Communication Clear and Inclusive

I will try to:

* Use simple and direct language.
* Provide enough context.
* Avoid unnecessary ambiguity.
* Ask questions instead of making assumptions.
* Respect different communication styles.
* Give people time to respond when something is not urgent.
* Clearly communicate when something is urgent.
* Make sure my messages explain what action or information I need.

Before sending an important message, I can ask myself:

**"Would another person clearly understand what I mean and what I need from them?"**

# 6. Creating a Positive and Professional Environment

I can contribute to a positive work environment by:

* Being friendly and respectful.
* Recognising teammates' contributions.
* Helping when I can.
* Sharing useful information.
* Being open to learning.
* Taking responsibility for my work.
* Keeping a positive attitude when facing problems.
* Being comfortable admitting when I do not know something.
* Participating in team conversations and activities.

Professionalism and a positive atmosphere are not opposites. A team can work seriously while still being relaxed and enjoyable.

---

# 7. Preparing for Meetings

Before a meeting with a supervisor or colleague, I should:

* Review the relevant task or issue.
* Know what I have completed.
* Identify anything I am currently working on.
* Prepare questions in advance.
* Note any blockers or decisions I need help with.
* Have relevant documents or examples ready.
* Join on time.

During the meeting, I should listen actively and take notes when necessary.

After the meeting, I should make sure I understand any actions assigned to me.

---

# 8. Being Proactive During My Internship

Being proactive means taking ownership instead of waiting for someone to tell me every next step.

Examples include:

* Investigating a problem before asking for help.
* Looking for relevant documentation.
* Preparing questions instead of saying only that I am confused.
* Informing teammates about blockers early.
* Suggesting improvements when I notice opportunities.
* Following up on tasks that are waiting for information.
* Looking for additional ways to improve my QA skills.
* Documenting useful findings for the team.

However, being proactive does not mean making decisions outside my responsibility without communicating with the appropriate person.

---

# 9. Following Up and Escalating

If I need information or action from a teammate, I will first send a clear and polite message explaining:

* What I need.
* Why I need it.
* Whether there is a deadline.
* Any relevant context.

If I do not receive a response, I can send a polite follow-up after a reasonable amount of time.

For example:

> "Hi! Just following up on my previous message about [task]. I need this information to continue with the task. Please let me know when you have a chance."

If the issue is blocking important work and there is still no response, I would escalate it to the appropriate supervisor or manager.

For urgent or serious matters, I would escalate sooner rather than repeatedly waiting.

---

# 10. Things I Will Not Do

I will not:

* Disrespect or insult teammates.
* Participate in bullying, discrimination, or harassment.
* Deliberately ignore important communication.
* Blame others for my mistakes.
* Hide blockers until the last moment.
* Share confidential information inappropriately.
* Pretend to understand something when I do not.
* React aggressively to constructive criticism.
* Take credit for someone else's work.
* Make serious decisions outside my responsibility without communicating with the relevant person.

---

# 11. Major Professional Conduct Issues

If I observe a serious breach of professional conduct or another significant issue that cannot be appropriately resolved through normal communication, I should follow the company's escalation process.

As part of the onboarding requirements, I have noted where the CEO's contact information is available in the onboarding materials so that I can use the appropriate escalation route if a major issue requires it.

I would not use this escalation route for normal disagreements or minor issues that can be resolved through regular team communication.

During my internship, I want to follow these principles:

1. **Be respectful.**
2. **Communicate clearly.**
3. **Take responsibility.**
4. **Ask instead of assuming.**
