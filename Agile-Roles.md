# Roles in an Agile Team

## 1. Key Roles in an Agile Team

An Agile team usually includes people with different responsibilities who work together to deliver a useful and high-quality product.

The exact roles can vary depending on the organization and the Agile framework being used.

### Developers

Developers are responsible for designing, implementing, and maintaining the product.

Their responsibilities can include:

* Writing and reviewing code.
* Implementing new features.
* Fixing bugs.
* Maintaining existing functionality.
* Working with QA to investigate and resolve defects.
* Considering technical risks and limitations.

Developers are closely connected to QA because QA needs to understand how a feature works, while developers need clear information about problems discovered during testing.

---

### QA (Quality Assurance)

QA is responsible for helping ensure that the product meets its requirements and provides a good experience for users.

QA responsibilities can include:

* Analyzing requirements.
* Creating test scenarios and test cases.
* Performing functional and exploratory testing.
* Testing new features and bug fixes.
* Performing regression testing.
* Reporting and documenting defects.
* Testing APIs and integrations when necessary.
* Identifying edge cases and potential risks.
* Communicating quality-related risks to the team.
* Verifying that fixes actually resolve reported issues.

QA is not only responsible for finding bugs. A QA engineer can contribute throughout the development process by asking questions, identifying risks early, and helping the team prevent defects.

---

### Product Manager (PM)

A Product Manager focuses on the product's goals, user needs, and business priorities.

Their responsibilities can include:

* Understanding customer needs.
* Defining and prioritizing product requirements.
* Managing and prioritizing the product backlog.
* Communicating product goals.
* Making decisions about product priorities.
* Working with different teams to ensure the product delivers value.

QA may collaborate with the PM to clarify requirements and understand the expected behaviour of a feature.

---

### Agile Project Manager (Agile PM)

An Agile Project Manager focuses more on facilitating the delivery process and helping the team work effectively.

Depending on the organization, this role may include:

* Coordinating project activities.
* Tracking progress.
* Identifying and communicating risks.
* Helping remove blockers.
* Supporting communication between teams.
* Helping ensure deadlines and priorities are understood.
* Improving team processes.

The responsibilities of an Agile PM can overlap with roles such as Scrum Master, project manager, or delivery manager depending on the organization's structure.

---

### Other Supporting Roles

Agile teams can also include other roles that contribute to the product.

#### UX/UI Designers

UX and UI designers focus on usability, user experience, and interface design.

QA can collaborate with UX/UI designers by checking whether the implemented interface matches the intended design and by identifying usability or accessibility issues.

#### Stakeholders

Stakeholders are people who have an interest in the product or are affected by its outcome.

They may provide requirements, business priorities, feedback, or constraints.

#### Customer Support

Customer support teams communicate directly with users and often receive information about real-world problems.

Their feedback can help QA and product teams identify recurring issues or areas that need improvement.

#### Customers/Users

Users are ultimately the people who interact with the product. Their feedback can help the team understand whether the product actually solves the intended problem.

# 2. Collaboration in Scrum vs. Kanban

The roles may be similar in both Scrum and Kanban, but the way people organize their work is different.

## Scrum

Scrum has a more defined structure.

The Scrum framework formally defines:

* Product Owner
* Scrum Master
* Developers

QA can be part of the Developers group rather than being a formally separate Scrum role.

The team usually works in fixed-length sprints. Collaboration happens through structured events such as Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, and Sprint Retrospective.

For example, QA may work with developers during a sprint to test completed functionality and provide feedback before the sprint ends.

## Kanban

Kanban focuses more on continuous workflow than fixed sprints.

There are no mandatory Scrum-style roles. People can have different responsibilities while working together around a shared workflow.

A typical workflow might look like:

`Backlog → To Do → In Progress → Review/Testing → Done`

QA may take a task when it reaches the testing stage, identify defects, communicate with developers, and move the work forward once the acceptance criteria are satisfied.

Because Kanban emphasizes continuous flow, priorities can be changed more easily when urgent bugs or new requirements appear.

# 3. My Role in the Agile Team

My role is **QA (Quality Assurance)**.

As a QA intern, my main responsibility is to help the team deliver reliable, functional, and user-friendly software.

My contribution includes:

* Understanding requirements and expected behaviour.
* Creating and executing test cases.
* Performing exploratory testing.
* Reporting bugs clearly.
* Retesting fixes.
* Performing regression testing when necessary.
* Thinking about edge cases.
* Communicating potential risks.
* Providing feedback about usability and user experience.
* Continuously improving my testing knowledge and process.

I see QA as a role that connects technical implementation with the user's perspective. A feature can technically work while still creating confusion or a poor user experience, so testing should consider more than just whether the code executes successfully.

---

# 4. How My Role Interacts With Other Roles

QA overlaps with several roles in an Agile team.

For example, QA and Product Management need to communicate about requirements and expected behaviour.

QA and Developers need to communicate about implementation details, defects, test results, and fixes.

QA can also collaborate with UX/UI designers when testing interfaces and usability.

This means that QA is not an isolated role. Good testing depends on communication and collaboration with the rest of the team.

---

# 5. Role I Frequently Collaborate With: Developers

The role I expect to collaborate with most frequently is **Developer**.

The relationship between QA and developers is important because both roles contribute to product quality from different perspectives.

Developers focus primarily on implementing functionality, while QA focuses on validating behaviour, identifying risks, and finding potential problems from different user and system perspectives.

A typical collaboration can look like this:

1. A developer implements a feature.
2. QA reviews the requirements and prepares test scenarios.
3. QA tests the feature.
4. QA identifies a potential defect.
5. QA reports the issue with clear reproduction steps and evidence.
6. The developer investigates and fixes the issue.
7. QA retests the fix.
8. QA checks related functionality when regression testing is necessary.
9. The task continues through the workflow once the expected behaviour is confirmed.

---

# 6. How I Can Improve Teamwork With Developers

I can improve collaboration with developers by making my communication **clear, objective, and useful**.

When reporting a bug, I should provide:

* A clear title.
* Steps to reproduce.
* Expected result.
* Actual result.
* Environment information.
* Relevant screenshots, videos, logs, or API responses when appropriate.
* Severity or impact when relevant.

I should describe the problem rather than blame the person who implemented the feature.

For example, instead of saying:

> "Your code is broken."

I should explain what happened and provide enough information for the developer to reproduce and investigate the issue.

I should also ask questions when I am unsure about the expected behaviour rather than making assumptions.

# 7. Personal Reflection

This research helped me understand that Agile teamwork is not about individual roles working separately. Each role contributes a different perspective to the same product.

As a QA, I can contribute more than just finding bugs. I can help identify risks early, ask questions about requirements, provide feedback, and help the team understand how a feature behaves from a user's perspective.

I also learned that good collaboration requires respect and clear communication. Developers and QA may sometimes have different perspectives about a defect, but the shared goal should be improving the product rather than proving who is right.

My personal goal is to become a QA engineer who is not only good at testing, but also a reliable and constructive team member.

**My principle for teamwork:**

> Communicate clearly, ask instead of assuming, and focus on solving the problem together.
