# Learning Goals

## What do I hope to learn from this internship?

One of my main goals for this internship is to understand what working as a QA tester in a real team actually looks like.

I already have experience with manual testing, writing test cases and bug reports, API testing, SQL, and some automation, but I want to understand how these skills are used in a real product with real users and a real development team.

I also want to become more comfortable with the full QA process — from understanding a requirement and exploring a feature to finding, investigating, documenting, and communicating a bug.

Most importantly, I want to learn from situations where there is no obvious answer and I have to investigate and make a decision myself.

## What skills am I most excited to develop?

I am especially excited to improve my exploratory testing and debugging skills.

I want to become better at looking at a feature from different perspectives and thinking about what could go wrong, rather than only following predefined test cases.

I also want to improve my ability to investigate bugs using browser DevTools, logs, network requests, and other technical information.

Another important skill for me is communication. I want to learn how to communicate with developers and other team members clearly, especially when I am not completely sure about the cause of a problem.

## What do I think I can contribute to the team?

I think I can contribute with curiosity, attention to detail, and a willingness to investigate things properly.

I enjoy looking for edge cases and asking questions when something does not make sense. I also try not to report something as a bug without first understanding what is actually happening.

Because I am interested in both QA and technology, I can also contribute from a more technical perspective when investigating issues. I am comfortable working with APIs, databases, browser tools, and basic automation, and I would like to continue developing these skills while contributing to the team.

I also think that being an intern does not mean I should only wait for instructions. If I notice something useful, have a question, or find a potential issue, I want to communicate it rather than ignore it.

## What is one area I feel less confident in that I’d like to improve?

One area I would like to improve is my confidence when working in an unfamiliar codebase or product.

When I do not know how something is implemented, it can sometimes take me longer to understand where a problem might be coming from. I want to become better at navigating unfamiliar systems, asking the right questions, and investigating without being afraid of making a mistake.

I also want to become more confident when communicating my technical findings to developers. I want my reports to be clear and useful rather than simply saying that something "doesn't work."

## What does success in this internship look like for me?

For me, success would not simply mean completing all the assigned tasks.

I would consider this internship successful if, by the end, I am much more confident working as part of a real QA team and can investigate problems independently.

I want to be able to take a feature, understand its purpose, think about possible risks, test it from different perspectives, and communicate my findings clearly.

I also want to look back and see that I have improved not only technically, but also in communication, teamwork, and confidence.

Ultimately, I want to finish the internship feeling that I am better prepared for my first Junior QA role and that I have contributed something useful to the team along the way.
